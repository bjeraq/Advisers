## Evergreen definitions
- **Pullback**: −5% to −10% from a high. **Correction**: −10% to −20% from the most recent record close. **Bear market**: −20% or more from the high. **Crash**: not an official threshold — a sudden, steep fall over days/weeks (1987: −20.5% in one day; 2020: −34% in 33 days). A correction is about depth; a crash is about speed.
- Measurement convention: closing levels vs the prior record close (media use closes).

## How different investor types typically behave (general, non-advice)
- **Long-term regular investor (monthly contributions / DCA)**: history shows declines are frequent and recoveries typical; many keep contributing on schedule, and avoid selling in panic. Risk: stopping contributions at lows.
- **Near retirement**: sequence risk matters most — a drop just before/at withdrawal hurts more. Commonly discussed: holding a cash/bond buffer (e.g., 1–3 years of spending) so stocks aren't sold at lows; reviewing allocation rather than reacting to headlines.
- **Trader / short-term**: volatility rises; typical practices are predefined stop-losses, smaller position sizes, avoiding leverage; corrections can include sharp bear-market rallies (e.g., +2.35% on 9 Nov 2026 inside this decline).
- Universal: nobody reliably knows if a correction becomes a bear; ~half the strongest rebound days historically occur near the worst days (general observation — don't quote a number).
