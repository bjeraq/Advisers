## Concepts (definitions, no numbers to verify)
- **Index fund / ETF:** holds every stock in an index (e.g. the 500 large US firms in the S&P 500) in index weights. It does not try to pick winners, which is why costs are low. An ETF trades on an exchange like a share.
- **Dollar-cost averaging (DCA):** invest a fixed amount on a fixed schedule (e.g. every salary day). You buy more units when prices are low and fewer when high, and you avoid trying to time the market.
  - Honest caveat: DCA does not guarantee a profit or protect against losses in a falling market. Also, lump-sum investing has historically beaten DCA more often than not, because markets rise more often than they fall.
- **Why fees matter:** the gap between 0.03% and 0.45–0.65% compounds over decades. Show it as an illustration, not a forecast.
